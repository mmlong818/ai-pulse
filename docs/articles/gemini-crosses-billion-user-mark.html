Gemini Reaches One Billion Monthly Active Users

Google’s AI assistant crossed one billion monthly users, matching ChatGPT and becoming its fastest-growing product to reach that scale.

A billion-user AI market

Google says the Gemini app has surpassed one billion monthly active users, up from more than 950 million at the end of June and 750 million in late 2025. The milestone makes Gemini the fastest-growing product in Google’s history to reach that level, according to the company. It also puts Gemini alongside ChatGPT, which reportedly crossed the same threshold in June.

The figure refers to the Gemini app rather than the much broader audiences encountering Gemini models through Google Search. Google has separately reported more than one billion monthly users for AI Mode and more than 2.5 billion for AI Overviews. Keeping those categories distinct matters: Gemini’s app audience represents people using a conversational assistant, while Search integrations can reach users without requiring them to adopt a standalone AI product.

Google’s distribution advantages remain central to the expansion. Gemini is available across Android, the web and Google’s productivity ecosystem, and is replacing the legacy Google Assistant on supported devices. New multimodal creation features, deeper connections to Gmail and other Google services, and prominent placement on Android have reduced the effort required to try the assistant. The company has not disclosed how frequently the average monthly user opens Gemini, how many pay for it, or whether its measurement is directly comparable with OpenAI’s.

Why it matters

One billion monthly users turns consumer AI from a fast-growing software category into mass infrastructure. The competitive question is no longer whether assistants can attract a mainstream audience, but which company can convert reach into habitual use, subscriptions, commerce and control of the interface through which people access other digital services. Google now has credible consumer scale alongside OpenAI while retaining Search, Android and Workspace as distribution channels. That combination could lower acquisition costs and make model quality only one part of the contest; integration, defaults and the ability to fund inference for a global audience are becoming equally decisive.
