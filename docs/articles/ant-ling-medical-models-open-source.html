Ant Group begins phased open-weight release of LingSante medical AI models

Ant’s LingSante family covers multimodal health understanding, medical reasoning, health-risk prediction, medical safety and real-time video consultation; the initial open-weight release includes two core models.

Five models for different clinical bottlenecks

Ant Group has announced the LingSante medical AI family through its Afu healthcare platform. The family separates tasks that are often bundled into one general assistant: multimodal health understanding, long-context medical reasoning, health-risk prediction, medical safety protection and real-time video consultation.

The initial open-weight release includes two core models. LingSante-3.1-air-VL is a lightweight native multimodal model with 124 billion total parameters and 5.5 billion active parameters per token. It supports text, image and video inputs and is positioned for localized or on-device health applications. LingSante-3.1-flash is designed for larger workloads, with 560 billion total parameters, about 25 billion active parameters per token and a context window of up to 1 million tokens. Ant says it supports evidence-based retrieval and long-horizon healthcare workflows.

Three specialized or application-oriented models complete the announced family. LingSante-Pulse is described as a health-prediction model that assesses risk for more than 150 conditions by integrating diverse health signals. LingSante-MedGuard focuses on medical safety, regulatory compliance and bioethical risks. LingSante-Live is a real-time multimodal agent integrated into the Afu app for video consultations and guidance. Ant’s announcement says the model weights will be released in phases, beginning with the two core models; it does not establish that Pulse, MedGuard or Live are already publicly downloadable as open-weight models.

Open weights, but not a clinical license

The announcement is significant because it packages capability and guardrails together rather than treating safety as a documentation layer around a general model. It also reflects a broader Asian push to make domain models deployable inside local institutions, where data residency and private hardware can matter as much as benchmark scores.

The claims should still be read carefully. Public reporting gives limited detail on training data, licensing terms, independent validation and regulatory clearance. A model that can interpret a report, estimate risk or support a video consultation is not automatically authorized to diagnose or prescribe. The value of the release will depend on whether hospitals and developers can audit the models, reproduce the reported behavior and maintain human review.

Uncle Cat take: The initial open-weight release covers the two core models, while Pulse, MedGuard and Live are separate parts of the announced family and application ecosystem. Without independent clinical validation, the health-risk forecast remains an ambition, not evidence of medical readiness.
