Google makes Gemini Tasks free across six international markets

Google is turning Gemini from a conversational assistant into an execution layer that can make calls, handle bills and complete approved tasks.

Google has begun rolling out Tasks in Gemini free of charge to hundreds of millions of users in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India. The feature lets people switch Gemini from Chat to Task mode and delegate multi-step personal work rather than merely request information.

From answers to execution

Google’s examples include making phone calls, paying bills, arranging appointments, adding events to calendars and handling actions from email. Users can also forward messages to gemini@google.com or interact through Google Messages and iMessage, turning the assistant into a task intake channel outside the main app.

The rollout is powered by Gemini 4 Hydrogen, while advanced features begin in the United States. Google says the system can use connected services and user-provided context to complete work, with the user remaining responsible for approval and final decisions where required.

That distinction matters. A chatbot’s value is measured by answer quality; an execution agent is judged by whether it can safely navigate accounts, forms, communications and payments. Each additional integration expands usefulness but also creates new failure modes around authorization, ambiguity, incorrect recipients and irreversible actions.

Distribution is the strategic advantage

Google is not introducing a standalone productivity product. Tasks is being placed inside an assistant already used by hundreds of millions of people, with entry points in the mobile app, email and messaging. Free access removes an important barrier to experimentation and gives Google a large real-world test bed for agent behavior.

The initial market list is also notable. It extends beyond the United States to major Asian and Pacific markets, suggesting Google is treating personal task automation as a global platform feature rather than a limited domestic experiment.

Why it matters

The announcement marks a practical shift in the assistant market: the key question is no longer whether an AI can suggest the next step, but whether it can complete the step across services. Google’s advantage is distribution; its unresolved challenge is earning enough trust for users to delegate consequential actions repeatedly.
