Sakana AI Adds Deeper Research Modes to Marlin

Sakana AI has upgraded its Marlin research agent with faster multi-angle analysis, broader discovery mode and redesigned PowerPoint export.

Sakana AI has updated its Marlin strategic research agent with two new modes, Twilight 2.0 and Midnight 2.0. The company says Twilight is designed for faster analysis from multiple angles, while Midnight is intended to search more broadly for less obvious findings. The update also substantially redesigns Marlin’s Microsoft PowerPoint export.

From answer generation to research workflow

Marlin retains its existing Sunlight mode as a lighter option. The new structure gives users a choice between speed, breadth and depth instead of treating research as a single generic prompt-and-answer task. Sakana describes the modes through an ocean metaphor: shallow, twilight and midnight zones represent progressively deeper investigation.

The PowerPoint changes are strategically important. Research agents often produce useful intermediate findings but fail at the last mile, where results must be organized into a document that executives, clients or colleagues can review. A stronger slide export turns Marlin from a conversational research interface into a workflow tool with a concrete business artifact at the end.

The announcement does not provide a standardized benchmark, pricing comparison or detailed account of the models and tools behind each mode. It therefore remains difficult to quantify how much of the improvement comes from better search, longer execution, improved orchestration or presentation-layer engineering.

Why it matters

Sakana is positioning Marlin around a Japanese and Asian research-automation use case rather than competing only on general chatbot capability. That makes the product relevant to consultants, analysts and strategy teams that need sourced exploration followed by a presentable deliverable.

The key question is whether the new modes reduce research time without increasing unsupported claims or bloated output. Better slide generation can improve adoption, but it also makes weak conclusions easier to circulate. Marlin’s importance will depend less on the names of its modes than on whether users can inspect evidence, trace the agent’s path and reliably distinguish discovery from verification.
