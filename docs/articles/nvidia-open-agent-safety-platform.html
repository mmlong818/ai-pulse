NVIDIA Unveils Hardware-Enforced Safety for AI Agents

NVIDIA introduced OpenShell and Sentry, a software-and-hardware stack designed to constrain, monitor and rapidly quarantine autonomous agents.

A safety layer outside the model

NVIDIA has launched the Open Agent Safety Platform, combining open-source runtime software with a reference hardware design for controlling AI agents from testing through deployment.

The platform’s software component, OpenShell, creates a runtime boundary around agents. It traces actions and enforces policies governing access to files, networks, processes, credentials, tools and external services. NVIDIA says OpenShell is broadly available and can be extended to third-party compute platforms from Arm and Intel.

The second component, Sentry, is an out-of-band watchdog designed to run on NVIDIA BlueField-4 data-processing units. It monitors agent behavior independently of the model and agent runtime, and can quarantine and stop an agent that crosses a defined boundary within milliseconds.

A broad ecosystem, with a hardware centre

NVIDIA says the launch includes leaders such as Anthropic, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Dell Technologies, Figure, HPE, Hugging Face, JPMorganChase, Microsoft, Palantir, Palo Alto Networks, Perplexity, Red Hat, Salesforce, SAP, Scale AI, ServiceNow and SpaceXAI. The company also says more than 100 organizations are working with its platform technologies. This ecosystem list is separate from the Open Secure AI Alliance, which NVIDIA says was initiated alongside more than 120 organizations.

The architecture reflects a growing concern that prompt rules and model-level safeguards are insufficient for long-running agents that can call tools, access enterprise data and retry after failures. External enforcement can reduce the consequences of a compromised or misbehaving agent, but Sentry’s strongest controls depend on NVIDIA’s networking hardware.

Why it matters

Agent safety is moving from policy documents into infrastructure procurement. The important question is no longer only whether a model refuses a dangerous request, but whether an enterprise can stop it after it begins operating across systems. NVIDIA’s platform could become a useful standard for that control layer, although adoption will depend on interoperability, measurable detection quality and whether customers accept hardware-linked security.

Uncle Cat take: OpenShell broadens access, but Sentry’s millisecond promise still ties the strongest containment story to BlueField-4 deployment economics.
