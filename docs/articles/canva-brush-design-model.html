Canva launches Brush for precise design editing

Canva has launched Brush, a vision-language model that edits layouts and multi-page designs, cutting reported task time by 69%.

What happened

Canva launched Brush, its first vision-language model built specifically for design editing. The model can inspect a design’s layout, layers and visual style, then apply instructions ranging from changing a single line of copy to revising an entire multi-page presentation.

Brush is available first to Canva Pro subscribers for supported edits in Canva AI. Users can ask it to restyle an image, apply a change across multiple pages or adapt an existing design to another format. Canva says Brush was built from a leading open-weights model and then post-trained on design tasks using professional design expertise, anonymized opt-in usage data and simulated interactions.

The company reports that Brush is up to 69% faster on general design-editing tasks and 46% cheaper per edit than its previous design agent. Canva says the model matched or exceeded the earlier system on most internal editing evaluations, particularly when restyling designs or adapting them for different channels. The first release does not yet support every planned workflow; brand-kit integration, attachments and stronger handling of image-heavy layouts are still in development.

Why it matters

Brush shows where application companies may gain leverage in generative AI: not by training the largest general model, but by optimizing a model around a structured professional workflow. Design files contain layers, constraints, reusable assets and formatting rules that ordinary image generators do not fully understand. A model that edits those objects directly can save users from rebuilding work after each generation.

The economics are also material. Canva serves hundreds of millions of users, so a 46% reduction in per-edit cost could affect whether advanced editing features are offered widely or restricted to premium tiers. The open question is how well Brush handles messy real-world documents, brand consistency and human review once usage expands beyond Canva’s internal tests.

Uncle Cat take

Brush’s 69% speed gain matters because Canva edits structured, multi-page work; its biggest risk is not image quality but breaking the design system users already rely on.
