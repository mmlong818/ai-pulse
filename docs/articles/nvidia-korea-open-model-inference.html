NVIDIA brings Korean open models to production with Dynamo

NVIDIA released deployment recipes for four Korean open models, aiming to make multi-node inference faster and easier to operationalize.

What launched

NVIDIA released Dynamo-optimized deployment recipes for four Korean open models during its AI Day Seoul cycle: SK Telecom’s A.X K2, LG AI Research’s K-EXAONE 2.0, Upstage’s Solar Open 2 and Motif Technologies’ Motif-3. The recipes are intended to help operators run models across multiple GPUs and nodes without building the serving stack from scratch.

Dynamo is NVIDIA’s open-source distributed inference framework. Its role is to coordinate request routing and inter-GPU communication when models exceed the capacity of one accelerator. NVIDIA says the new recipes are optimized for B200 systems and can deliver up to twice the performance out of the box, although the company did not present a neutral comparison covering different workloads, hardware configurations or serving costs.

The announcement is part of a broader Korean push for sovereign AI. South Korea is investing in domestic foundation models, local infrastructure and public-facing AI services, while its technology companies seek to compete beyond the home market. By publishing deployment templates for local models, NVIDIA is positioning its software layer as the bridge between national model programs and production-scale infrastructure.

Why it matters

This is more consequential than a routine model partnership because the bottleneck is increasingly deployment. A strong regional model still has limited impact if enterprises cannot serve it efficiently, scale it across nodes or integrate it into production systems. The recipes also give NVIDIA influence over the operational layer surrounding models that it did not create. That can strengthen its ecosystem position even when governments and companies want local model ownership.

The unresolved question is portability. If the recipes are tightly optimized around NVIDIA B200 hardware, they may accelerate Korean models while reinforcing dependence on one accelerator stack. Their long-term value will be clearer when the same models run competitively on Korean and alternative hardware.

Uncle Cat take

The four named Korean models matter less than Dynamo’s attempt to become the default production bridge between sovereign models and NVIDIA infrastructure.
