Liquid AI releases two open 8K-context CPU encoders

LFM2.5-Encoder-230M and 350M are bidirectional encoders built from Liquid's decoders, claiming a 3x speedup over ModernBERT-base at 8K tokens on CPU.

Liquid AI has published open weights for two small bidirectional encoders, LFM2.5-Encoder-230M and LFM2.5-Encoder-350M, both with an 8,192-token context window and both available on Hugging Face.

The models

Rather than training encoders from scratch, Liquid converted its existing LFM2.5 decoder checkpoints. The recipe applies bidirectional attention masking, swaps the causal short convolutions for non-causal ones, and continues training as a masked language model at a 30% masking ratio. The result is a pair of general-purpose base encoders covering 15 languages, intended to be fine-tuned into task-specific heads for classification, token classification, retrieval, reranking and semantic similarity — the workloads that still dominate production NLP even as generative models absorb attention and budget.

The 230M variant targets tight latency and memory budgets; the 350M is positioned for maximum downstream quality. The performance claim Liquid leads with is long-context throughput on commodity hardware: at 8K tokens on CPU, the 230M model completes a forward pass in roughly 28 seconds against more than 90 seconds for ModernBERT-base. The company reports competitive results across natural language inference, paraphrase detection, sentiment and multilingual benchmarks.

Why it matters

Encoder models are the unglamorous load-bearing layer of deployed AI. Retrieval pipelines, rerankers, content classifiers and safety filters run at far higher request volumes than the generative models they serve, and they run under latency budgets measured in milliseconds. BERT-family architectures have held that position for years largely because nothing smaller and faster arrived with comparable quality and a usable context window.

The specific gap Liquid is aiming at is long-context inference without a GPU. Most retrieval and classification stacks either truncate aggressively at 512 tokens or pay for accelerators they otherwise would not need. An encoder that stays usable at 8K on CPU changes the cost structure for on-device deployments and for the large tail of enterprises running document classification on ordinary servers. It is a narrow release with no frontier-scale headline — and, precisely for that reason, the kind that quietly ends up in a great deal of shipped infrastructure.
