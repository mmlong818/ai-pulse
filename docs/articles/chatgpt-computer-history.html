ChatGPT Gains an Opt-In Memory of Computer Activity

OpenAI’s Computer History lets ChatGPT recall recent activity across Mac apps and websites, opening a new front in personal context.

From conversation memory to computer memory

OpenAI has begun rolling out Computer History, an opt-in feature that allows ChatGPT to use a record of recent activity across applications and websites on a Mac. Instead of relying only on chats, uploaded files and connected services, the assistant can draw on a broader timeline of what the user has recently been doing and resume work with less repeated explanation.

The feature is launching in the ChatGPT desktop application for Mac for Pro, Business and Enterprise customers. OpenAI says availability is rolling out globally, although access in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland will follow later. Users enable it through the app’s Integrations settings rather than having their activity captured by default.

Computer History builds on OpenAI’s earlier Chronicle research preview. The company says the production version uses fewer tokens and adds privacy controls, including a timeline where people can inspect and remove recorded activity. OpenAI has not presented it as unlimited long-term memory; its immediate purpose is to supply recent working context to future ChatGPT and Codex interactions.

The distinction matters because a chronological record of computer use can contain far more sensitive information than an ordinary chat history: documents opened, sites visited and fragments of work spanning multiple applications. An opt-in design, visible timeline and deletion controls reduce some risk, but organizations will still need to decide whether the productivity gain justifies exposing this category of context to an assistant. The delayed European rollout also indicates that regional privacy and compliance requirements remain operational constraints.

Why it matters

Computer History moves the AI assistant closer to being a persistent layer over the operating system. If it works reliably, users may no longer need to manually assemble context before delegating a task, removing one of the largest sources of friction in agent workflows. At the same time, it makes control over collection, retention and deletion a core product battleground. The companies that win personal AI may be those trusted to remember the most—not merely those with the strongest underlying model.
