X Square releases WALL-OSS-1.0 for zero-shot robot control

X Square’s new embodied model unifies language, vision and action during pretraining, reporting 55.1% zero-shot success across real-robot tests.

X Square Robot has released WALL-OSS-1.0, an embodied foundation model that puts robot-action data into the pretraining process alongside language and visual data. The company says the model is designed to follow open-ended instructions and operate unfamiliar objects, layouts and tools without task-specific demonstrations or fine-tuning.

A unified training recipe

WALL-OSS-1.0 starts from Qwen3.5 and represents language, images, robot states and actions in a shared autoregressive framework. Continuous robot trajectories are converted into discrete action tokens, allowing data from different embodiments—including single arms, dual arms and mobile platforms—to contribute to one training mixture. X Square says the system uses more than hundreds of thousands of hours of interaction trajectories and millions of operation records, with a large share collected through operator-worn UMI devices.

The model family also includes Lite and Nano variants for lower-cost adaptation and deployment, plus Gen, which learns from egocentric human video through generative supervision when precise robot action labels are unavailable. X Square says Nano can support 18 robots on one consumer Blackwell GPU in production-style scenarios.

Results and limits

The company reports a 55.1% zero-shot success rate across real-robot tests and says WALL-OSS-1.0 leads or ties on 14 of 15 listed manipulation tasks without post-pretraining fine-tuning. It also reports a 79.2% average on eight general-capability benchmarks, close to 79.4% for the original base model, suggesting that adding action training did not materially erase broad multimodal ability.

Those figures remain company-reported results, and the breadth of the test environments, hardware, task definitions and failure costs will determine how far they generalize. Still, the release matters because it makes embodied pretraining—not merely downstream robot fine-tuning—the central product and research claim. If the weights and tooling are usable outside X Square’s own hardware stack, WALL-OSS-1.0 could give open robotics developers a more credible starting point for cross-embodiment control.
