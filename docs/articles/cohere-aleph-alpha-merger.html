Cohere and Aleph Alpha Sign Definitive Sovereign AI Merger Agreement

Cohere and Aleph Alpha have signed a definitive merger agreement, creating a Canada-Germany AI group aimed at regulated and sovereign deployments; final financial terms were not disclosed.

Cohere and Germany’s Aleph Alpha have signed a definitive business-combination agreement, converting an earlier plan into a formal transaction. The combined company will keep the Cohere name and operate with headquarters in Toronto and Berlin, while Aleph Alpha’s Heidelberg site is expected to remain a research center.

The companies position the deal around sovereign AI: models and enterprise systems that governments and regulated industries can deploy with stronger control over data, infrastructure, and jurisdiction. The transaction remains subject to final regulatory approvals and other closing conditions. Earlier reports placed the prospective combination’s valuation at about $20 billion, but the definitive announcement did not disclose final financial terms.

The strategic logic is broader than adding model capabilities. Cohere brings a larger international enterprise business and model-development platform; Aleph Alpha contributes European government relationships and a strong emphasis on controlled deployment. Schwarz Group, Aleph Alpha’s major backer, is also advancing its partnership with the combined company around Germany’s STACKIT sovereign cloud ecosystem.

The merger matters because sovereign AI is becoming a purchasing requirement rather than a political slogan. Middle powers increasingly want alternatives to dependence on American hyperscalers or Chinese providers, especially in public administration, defense, finance, and healthcare. The unresolved question is whether a cross-border company can deliver genuinely local control while still achieving the scale needed to compete with frontier labs. Its first test will be turning governance assurances and regional infrastructure into contracts that customers can actually deploy.
