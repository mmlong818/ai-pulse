Amazon Agrees to Buy Decart Software to Challenge Nvidia’s Lead

Amazon’s reported $7.5 billion Decart deal targets the software layer that could make Trainium chips more competitive with Nvidia, but the transaction remains subject to closing conditions.

The deal

Amazon has signed an agreement to purchase the proprietary technology behind Decart’s AI optimization stack and bring Decart’s three co-founders and AI research and engineering team to Amazon. The transaction has been reported at $7.5 billion, although Amazon’s announcement does not state a purchase price and says completion remains subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance.

The purchase centers on Decart Optimization Stack, software designed to improve how AI workloads run across hardware platforms. Amazon says Decart’s full-stack approach can help optimize inference and custom-silicon performance, including Trainium.

Decart became known publicly for interactive world models and real-time video generation, but Amazon is acquiring a less visible capability: the ability to extract more useful work from each accelerator. Amazon says the technology could support faster training, reinforcement learning and inference across its custom silicon portfolio.

Why Trainium matters

Amazon has developed Trainium as part of its custom-silicon portfolio and delivers access to it through AWS. It is continuing to expand the availability of its custom chips to customers, putting pressure on Nvidia’s dominant position in AI infrastructure. Hardware alone is not enough to shift workloads. Customers also need compilers, kernels, scheduling, profiling and optimization tools that make a new accelerator predictable to use.

That is where Decart fits. Amazon says the two companies have worked together since 2025 and achieved performance results on Trainium through a full-stack software-and-hardware approach. The agreement would also bring Amazon a specialized team with experience optimizing demanding generative workloads.

What remains unproven

The proposed transaction is not a guarantee that Trainium will become a credible Nvidia substitute. Amazon still has to close the deal, integrate Decart’s technology into AWS and its custom-silicon efforts, maintain compatibility with changing model architectures and persuade customers that performance gains are repeatable outside internal tests. Regulatory review, team retention and integration risks remain material.

Still, the strategic signal is unusually clear. A reported $7.5 billion valuation would represent a platform-scale bet, but the final price and completion status should not be treated as settled until Amazon closes the transaction. The company is treating software efficiency as the decisive battleground for its chip business—and as the bridge between owning silicon and selling an ecosystem.
