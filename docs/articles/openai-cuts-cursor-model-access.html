OpenAI to Cut Cursor Access After SpaceX Acquisition

OpenAI plans to stop supplying models directly to Cursor, turning a change-of-control clause into a major platform split.

OpenAI invokes its change-of-control rights

OpenAI has notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models directly to Cursor, proposing November 12, 2026, as the cutoff. The company said this is the longest notice permitted under a change-of-control provision triggered by SpaceX’s acquisition of Cursor.

OpenAI framed the decision as a question of contractual trust and model governance. It cited previous alleged violations of its terms by companies controlled by Elon Musk and said those experiences left it unable to rely on SpaceX following the same safeguards. The announcement also draws a line around Astra, OpenAI’s forthcoming model: Cursor can retain the OpenAI models covered during the transition, but OpenAI says it will not provide future models through the existing partnership.

The final termination date has not yet been confirmed by both companies, and Cursor could end access earlier. OpenAI’s support guidance says users should still be able to run its models inside Cursor by supplying their own API key, using the Codex extension, or connecting through compatible gateways such as Azure or Amazon Bedrock. The rupture therefore concerns Cursor’s direct commercial access rather than a universal technical block.

Why it matters

Cursor is both a major distribution channel for frontier models and an increasingly important model builder in its own right. SpaceX’s acquisition already aligned the coding platform with Grok and large-scale proprietary compute; losing automatic access to OpenAI’s next generation accelerates that vertical integration. It may also change expectations across the agent market: model suppliers can treat an acquisition by a competitor as grounds to reconsider access even when end users retain indirect routes. Developers are unlikely to lose OpenAI compatibility overnight, but Cursor must now replace a strategic supplier while proving that its multi-model experience will survive a more fragmented ownership landscape.
