US Orders DeepSeek Removed From Federal Civilian Systems

A DHS order bars civilian agencies from buying DeepSeek technology and requires removal of third-party products built on its implementations.

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has ordered federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products and related implementations from their information-technology systems. The notice also excludes DeepSeek and affiliated products from future civilian-agency procurement, extending the restriction to third-party services developed using DeepSeek technology.

What the order covers

The action was published through the Federal Register under the Federal Acquisition Security Council framework. It applies to civilian executive-branch agencies rather than the general public, and it is not a blanket prohibition on private companies or consumers using DeepSeek. The order cites supply-chain and national-security concerns, including the possibility of sensitive information being transferred to China and alleged software-security risks.

The wording is broader than a simple ban on visiting DeepSeek’s public chatbot. Agencies must remove DeepSeek products, affiliated offerings and third-party tools that incorporate DeepSeek implementations. That could affect contractors and internal applications even when DeepSeek is not the visible brand presented to employees.

The timing is important. The order itself was issued earlier, but its Federal Register publication makes the restriction part of the government’s formal procurement and technology-removal record during this edition window. China criticized the move, framing it as another escalation in the technology conflict between Washington and Beijing.

Why it matters

The immediate commercial impact is concentrated in the U.S. federal market, not the global developer community. Its broader significance is precedent: Washington is treating a widely distributed open model as a supply-chain risk and extending restrictions to downstream products built on it. That approach could complicate compliance for contractors and encourage governments to demand provenance records for model weights and inference services. It also creates a sharper split between technical availability and institutional usability: DeepSeek may remain easy to download, while becoming harder to deploy in regulated public-sector environments.
