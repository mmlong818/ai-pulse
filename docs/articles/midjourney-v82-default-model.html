Midjourney makes V8.2 its default image model

Midjourney promoted V8.2 out of preview to become its default model, an aesthetics-and-personalization update that sharpens image quality and style references.

What changed

Midjourney announced on July 24 that V8.2 is now its default image model, graduating from the preview state it had occupied since late June. The company framed the release as an aesthetics-focused upgrade rather than a new architecture: the emphasis is on image quality, personalization, and improved style-reference (Sref) behavior, alongside a new stylize control. Existing users are switched to V8.2 automatically, though the older V8.1 remains selectable.

The pitch

During its preview run, V8.2 was described by testers as producing cleaner, more coherent images with fewer of the small artifacts that betray AI generation, and with a look that steps away from the divisive V7 aesthetic. Midjourney positioned personalization — tuning outputs to an individual creator's taste profile — as a central theme, continuing its bet that a distinctive, tunable house style is the product moat rather than raw benchmark supremacy.

Company context

The default-model switch caps a busy week for Midjourney, which also disclosed it is acquiring the astrology app Co-Star and installing its CEO as chief design officer to build out a cross-functional design and product team. Together the moves signal a company investing heavily in taste, interface and brand as image-generation quality across the industry converges.

Why it matters

Default-model changes at Midjourney reach a large paying creator base instantly and reset the reference point for consumer image quality. In a field where FLUX, Seedream, Recraft and others are pushing rapidly on fidelity, Midjourney is competing on aesthetic identity and personalization — a reminder that in mature AIGC categories, differentiation is shifting from capability to craft and user experience.
