Suno Studio 2.0 Adds Chat, MIDI and Custom Effects

Suno is turning its music generator into an editable production environment, giving creators finer control over AI-made tracks.

From song generation to production workflow

Suno has introduced Studio 2.0, expanding its product beyond prompt-to-song generation into a more conventional music-production environment. The release adds conversational editing, MIDI support and custom audio effects, giving musicians more ways to modify individual elements instead of repeatedly regenerating an entire track.

The new chat interface lets users describe changes in ordinary language while working inside a project. Rather than treating the prompt as a one-time instruction, creators can ask the system to adjust parts of an arrangement as the track develops. Suno is presenting the assistant as a collaborator embedded in the studio, closer to communicating with a bandmate or engineer than operating a standalone chatbot.

MIDI support is an important bridge to established production practices. MIDI represents musical notes and performance instructions rather than fixed audio, allowing pitches, timing and instruments to be edited precisely or transferred between compatible tools. Custom effects further extend that control by letting users shape how a track sounds instead of accepting the model’s initial mix and texture as final.

The release reflects a broader change in generative media products. Early music systems were judged primarily on whether a short prompt could produce an impressive finished song. Professional and serious hobbyist workflows require a different standard: generated material must remain editable, interoperable and responsive to detailed creative direction. Studio 2.0 attempts to place generation inside that longer process.

Why it matters

Suno is competing not only with other music generators but increasingly with digital audio workstations and the AI features being added to them. Moving upstream into editing can increase retention, make the product useful for longer projects and reduce the incentive to export generated audio immediately. It also raises the stakes around attribution, rights and professional adoption, because AI-generated material can now be interwoven more deeply with human composition and production. The central AIGC contest is shifting from who can generate the best first draft to who gives creators the strongest control after generation.
