Anthropic prices IPO at $83 ahead of Nasdaq debut

Anthropic priced its public offering above the expected range, putting the Claude maker at the center of the AI industry’s capital race.

A landmark public-market test

Anthropic said it priced its Class A initial public offering at $83 per share, above the previously indicated $70–$78 range. The company plans to begin trading on Nasdaq on November 19 under the symbol ANT.

The offering comprises 980 million Class A shares, including shares sold by Anthropic and existing stockholders. The company also granted underwriters an option to purchase up to 147 million additional shares. Based on the reported share count and pricing, Anthropic is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

The IPO comes as frontier-model companies face extraordinary infrastructure costs. Anthropic’s prospectus describes major commitments to compute, data-center capacity and strategic financing, while Epoch AI said the company spent about $10 billion on compute and infrastructure in the third quarter. Anthropic’s public listing therefore offers investors direct exposure to the economics of frontier AI, but also exposes the company to quarterly scrutiny over losses, capacity commitments and model margins.

Why it matters

This is more than a financing event. Anthropic is the first major frontier-model lab to enter public markets at this scale, creating a daily price signal for the value of model companies and their supply-chain partners. The $83 pricing suggests strong demand despite wider concerns about AI capital intensity. It also raises the pressure to show that Claude’s enterprise growth can eventually outpace the cost of training and serving increasingly capable systems.

The first trading sessions will matter less as a verdict on Anthropic’s technology than as a test of whether public investors accept frontier AI’s unusually long and expensive path to profitability.

Uncle Cat take: The $83 price validates investor appetite, but Anthropic’s real liability is the compute bill that public markets can now inspect every quarter.
