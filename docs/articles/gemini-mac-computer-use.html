Google gives Gemini control of Mac apps and websites

Gemini’s Mac app can now click, type, and complete workflows across desktop software, local files, and websites in the background.

Google has begun rolling out computer use in the Gemini app for Mac, allowing the assistant to navigate desktop applications, local files, and websites on a user’s behalf. The feature can click controls, type into forms, and move through multi-step workflows while running in an isolated window, according to Google.

The launch is initially limited to users in the United States who use English and subscribe to Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra. It therefore represents a meaningful product release, but not yet a broad platform capability. Users still retain control of the screen, and Google presents the feature as a way to automate repetitive work such as organizing files, transferring information between applications, or completing web forms.

From chat to operating-system action

Computer-use systems are different from ordinary assistants that call APIs or operate inside a single service. They interact with the same visual interfaces people use, which lets them handle software that lacks a dedicated integration. That flexibility also creates a wider failure surface: an incorrect click can modify a document, send a message, or submit a transaction.

Google’s Mac release places the company directly in competition with other efforts to make agents operate personal computers. It also arrives as Apple is reportedly hiring engineers to build its own desktop agent, making control of the operating system an increasingly important battleground rather than a niche accessibility feature.

Why it matters

The significance is practical rather than benchmark-driven. If Gemini can reliably execute mundane cross-application work, the value of an assistant shifts from answering questions to completing tasks. But the restricted rollout and requirement for broad permissions show that reliability, authorization, and recovery from mistakes remain as important as raw model intelligence.

Uncle Cat take

Google’s isolated-window design is the important detail: the product is selling background execution while keeping the user’s Mac visibly recoverable.
