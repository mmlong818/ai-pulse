Kimi adds Gmail, Drive and Calendar connectivity

Moonshot says Kimi can now work across Gmail, Google Drive and Calendar, turning the assistant from a chat interface into a cross-app work layer.

Kimi moves into the user’s work graph

Moonshot has announced that Kimi can connect with Gmail, Google Drive and Google Calendar. The assistant can summarize email threads, search files and help plan a user’s day across the three services. The announcement was posted during the Asian daytime cycle, making it one of the clearest fresh product moves in this edition’s regional window.

The feature is strategically important because it changes where Kimi can create value. A standalone chatbot competes mainly on model quality and conversation. A connected assistant competes on whether it can understand the relationships among messages, documents, schedules and pending actions. That puts Kimi closer to the emerging “personal work layer” category, where retrieval and action across applications matter as much as raw reasoning scores.

Integration is the product, permission is the risk

Cross-application context can make routine tasks substantially shorter. A user could ask Kimi to locate a project document, summarize the related email discussion and identify calendar constraints before proposing a plan. The same workflow also creates a much higher bar for authorization, data isolation and action confirmation. The announcement does not yet establish the full permission model, supported account tiers, regional availability or whether Kimi can make changes directly or only prepare recommendations.

That uncertainty matters. Access to a mailbox and calendar is not merely another tool call; it gives an assistant a live view of a person’s commitments, relationships and unfinished work. Kimi’s move therefore deserves attention less as a feature checklist than as a test of whether Chinese AI products can build globally relevant productivity ecosystems around third-party services.

Uncle Cat take

The headline is three connectors, but the real asset is the combined context of inbox, files and time. Moonshot’s next challenge is proving that this context stays under user control.
