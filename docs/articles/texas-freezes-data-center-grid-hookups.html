Texas freezes new data center grid hookups pending audit

Governor Greg Abbott ordered ERCOT and state regulators to audit every new data center before it can connect, with 474GW now queued.

A mandatory audit replaces a voluntary survey

Texas Governor Greg Abbott on August 3 directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas and grid operator ERCOT to put new data center projects through a formal audit before they can be granted an interconnection to the state grid. Until a project clears that review, its request to connect is effectively on hold.

The order lands on an interconnection queue that has roughly doubled in seven months. ERCOT was holding about 233 gigawatts of pending connection requests in January; by August that figure had reached roughly 474 gigawatts, of which about 90 percent is attributed to data centers. The queue now represents more than five times the grid's all-time peak demand — a gap wide enough that regulators cannot treat the requests as a credible forecast of what will actually be built.

What developers must now disclose

Under the audit, operators are required to hand over details they were previously free to withhold: projected electricity draw, water consumption, noise mitigation plans, outdoor lighting controls, which state and local tax incentives they are claiming, and who ultimately owns the project. Abbott had earlier asked for much of this through a voluntary information request, which drew almost no participation — the mandatory process is the direct consequence of that failure.

Why it matters

Texas has been the single most attractive destination for US AI compute construction, precisely because ERCOT's standalone grid and light-touch permitting let projects move faster than in PJM or California. A screening gate on that grid removes the industry's main escape valve at the moment when frontier labs are signing multi-gigawatt, multi-year compute contracts on the assumption that power can be found. Speculative filings will be squeezed out, which improves planning quality, but genuine projects also inherit new timeline risk and disclosure obligations. Expect the cost of firm, near-term capacity to rise, and expect more announcements to point at Nordic hydro, the Gulf, and the Middle East rather than West Texas.
