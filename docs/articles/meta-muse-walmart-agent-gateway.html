Walmart lets Meta Muse complete Black Friday purchases

Walmart’s Agent Gateway moves AI shopping into production, letting Meta Muse search, build carts and check out under user-set limits.

An agent gets a sanctioned checkout lane

Walmart has activated its Agent Gateway with support for Meta’s Muse, allowing the assistant to search Walmart’s catalog, assemble a basket and complete purchases for Black Friday deals. The deployment turns an agent-commerce interface announced previously into a live retail workflow at the start of the holiday shopping season.

The system uses Walmart’s Agent Profiles as a permission boundary inside a customer account. Users set spending limits, approve purchases when required and review a record of what Muse bought. Checkout uses a payment card already stored with Walmart, while Walmart says the agent does not need access to the shopper’s broader purchase history and that agent-driven orders receive the same prices and return policy as ordinary purchases.

The integration is deliberately narrower than giving an agent unrestricted browser access. Muse can ask Walmart’s systems for product information and act through the retailer’s approved interface, reducing the fragility of screen scraping and making it easier for Walmart to define what an outside agent may do.

A platform choice with industry consequences

Walmart says Muse is only the first supported agent and that additional shopping experiences are planned. The move therefore matters beyond one Black Friday promotion: it gives retailers a template for opening inventory and checkout to third-party assistants while preserving control over identity, payment and transaction logs.

It also exposes the split forming in online commerce. Walmart is building a controlled entrance for outside agents, while Amazon and other platforms have taken a more defensive stance toward Muse. The unresolved issues are conversion quality, fraud, returns, sponsored placement and whether retailers can keep agents from turning product discovery into a new form of opaque advertising.

Uncle Cat take: Agent Profiles and spending caps are the real product here; Walmart is testing whether permissioned checkout can attract demand without surrendering the customer relationship to Muse.
