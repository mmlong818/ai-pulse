Alibaba introduces Qwen Intelligence for mobile agents

Alibaba unveiled Qwen Intelligence with mobile planning, device-use and creative agents, aiming to make smartphone assistants act across apps.

What happened

Alibaba’s Qwen team introduced Qwen Intelligence, a mobile-focused agent suite built around three systems: Mobile Planner Agent, Mobile-Use Agent and Mobile Creative Agent. The announcement was made through Qwen’s official account, which published separate benchmark and performance links for each component.

The product is designed to move beyond answering questions inside a chat window. A planner can decompose a user request into steps, a mobile-use agent can operate interfaces and applications, and a creative agent can execute content-production tasks on a phone. Alibaba presented the group as a way to bring “personal intelligence” to smartphones, rather than as another general-purpose chatbot release.

The distinction matters because mobile agents face constraints that browser-based assistants can often avoid: small screens, changing layouts, permission prompts, latency, battery use and the risk of taking an irreversible action inside an app. Alibaba’s launch materials emphasize benchmark performance, but the public announcement does not yet provide enough independent detail to establish how reliably the agents handle long, messy, real-world tasks.

Why it matters

If the system works at consumer scale, the smartphone becomes an execution surface for AI rather than merely a place where users ask questions. That could shift competition toward operating-system access, app permissions and partnerships with handset makers. It also places more pressure on mobile platforms to define what agents may read, click, purchase or send on a user’s behalf.

The immediate significance is strategic rather than technical proof. Alibaba is tying Qwen to device-level action at a time when major assistants are racing to connect models with external services. The unresolved question is whether Qwen Intelligence can maintain user trust when a plan crosses multiple apps, especially in markets where local services and device ecosystems determine what an agent can actually do.
