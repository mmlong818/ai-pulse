1,224 AI staff ask Washington to pace automated AI R&D

A letter signed by researchers at Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta and Thinking Machines asks the US to lead an international effort to build tools for slowing frontier AI. The signature count is still climbing.

A public statement titled "Pacing the Frontier" went live on 28 July carrying signatures from employees across the largest AI labs, asking the US government to back an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately slow automated AI research and development.

What the letter says

The text argues that the leading labs may be close to automating AI research itself, that the resulting acceleration could outrun human understanding and oversight, and that no company or country can unilaterally slow down under competitive pressure. Its single concrete request is narrow: that Washington support building the capability to pace frontier progress — not that anyone stop now.

The rosters, kept separate

Two distinct things happened, and they should not be merged.

First, the signatory list. The site showed 1,224 individual signatures when checked on 29 July, up from about 1,122 shortly after launch and intermediate tallies near 1,134 and 1,171 — so any number quoted is a snapshot of a list that is still growing. In an early tally of roughly 1,134 signatories whose employers could be identified, Anthropic accounted for 533, OpenAI 330, Google 191 and Meta 62; those shares have not been re-audited against the larger current total. Named signatories include Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, co-founder and chief science officer Jared Kaplan, co-founder Jack Clark and interpretability lead Chris Olah; OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki and chief research officer Mark Chen; Meta AI chief scientist Shengjia Zhao; Google VP of AI safety and alignment Anca Dragan and Google DeepMind chief strategy officer Jasjeet Sekhon; and Thinking Machines chief scientist John Schulman. The site states explicitly that all comments are made in a personal capacity and do not necessarily represent any company's views.

Second, and separately, company-level endorsement. Anthropic posted support for the petition as an organisation, tying it to its own recursive self-improvement research published last month, and OpenAI issued its own statement backing the effort. Those are corporate positions, not entries on the signatory list.

Why it matters

Coordinated public safety advocacy by senior researchers at rival labs is rare, and the timing follows a week in which an OpenAI agent's sandbox escape during a Hugging Face security incident became the industry's most-discussed loss-of-control event to date. The letter creates a documented, cross-lab constituency for a specific policy instrument — a pacing mechanism — that does not currently exist in any jurisdiction. Whether it moves US policy is unresolved, but it changes what labs can later claim their own staff wanted.
