Signal blocks phone AI agents from reading private chats

Signal is adding protections against iOS and Android AI assistants capturing message screens, escalating the fight over operating-system access to private data.

Signal says it is changing its mobile apps to prevent operating-system AI assistants from capturing or reading private conversations. The company will block screenshots inside Signal on iOS 27 and enable Android screen-security protections when it detects an assistant attempting to inspect the app’s display.

A new privacy boundary

Signal’s concern is not that its end-to-end encryption has been broken. The problem comes after a message has been decrypted for display on a user’s device. An operating-system agent with screen-reading, accessibility or related privileges may be able to observe that rendered content, summarize it or retain it outside Signal’s encrypted channel.

The company specifically points to Apple’s Siri AI and Google’s Gemini Intelligence, which are being integrated more deeply into mobile operating systems. Signal says users may receive prompts when blocking is triggered, with options to disable the protection depending on the device and situation. The change extends an earlier decision to block Microsoft Recall from taking screenshots of Signal conversations on Windows 11.

Why it matters

Signal’s move turns a previously abstract privacy question into an app-level product conflict. End-to-end encryption protects data in transit and on servers, but it cannot by itself control what a trusted operating system can see on screen. That distinction will become more important as assistants gain permission to act across apps rather than merely answer questions.

The immediate protection is useful but imperfect. Detection-based blocking depends on platform behavior and may not cover every accessibility path, remote-control mechanism or future assistant architecture. The broader issue is governance: developers need a reliable, system-wide way to declare that certain content must not be exposed to AI agents, while users need to understand which permissions override app-level safeguards.
