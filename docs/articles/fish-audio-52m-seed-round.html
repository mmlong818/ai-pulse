Fish Audio raises $52M seed at $21M ARR for voice models

The speech-generation startup that began as one researcher's single-GPU side project now reports 8 million users and is keeping its newest model closed.

Fish Audio has closed a $52 million seed round co-led by Coreline Ventures and Capital Today, with participation from 359 Capital, Parable, Play Time, Alphalist Partners, Bayhouse Ventures, Carya Venture Partners and HF0.

From side project to revenue

The company grew out of work by Shijia Liao, a former Nvidia researcher who trained a voice-generation model on a single GPU and released it openly. The resulting Fish Speech repository has accumulated more than 31,000 GitHub stars and a following among independent developers, game studios and creators — an unusually strong open-source base for a company that had not raised institutional capital.

Fish Audio now reports $21 million in annual recurring revenue and more than eight million users across creators, developers and enterprises. It has shipped five models in the past year: four for speech generation and one for speech recognition. Three of the speech-generation models are open, but the newest, S2.1 Pro, is available only through the paid API. The capital is earmarked for further model development and for infrastructure to absorb enterprise demand, with an audio-understanding model planned for later this year and a speech-to-speech model in development.

Why it matters

Voice is the most commercially settled corner of generative media. Where image and video models still struggle to convert attention into recurring revenue, speech synthesis sells into dubbing, game production, accessibility, audiobooks and — increasingly — the voice layer of conversational agents, where ElevenLabs has been building a competing position. A seed round of this size against $21 million in existing ARR reflects that the category has stopped being speculative.

The more instructive detail is the licensing split. Fish Audio used open weights to acquire distribution and credibility, then withheld its best model to monetise it. That sequence — open the base, close the frontier — is becoming the default playbook for small labs in generative media, and it is a quieter but more durable version of the open-weights debate now consuming the frontier labs. It also raises the obvious question the company has not yet answered: whether the open repositories that built its audience continue to receive its best work.
