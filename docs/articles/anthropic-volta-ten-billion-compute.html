Anthropic buys $10B of compute from six-month-old Volta

The six-year deal routes Anthropic through a 133MW hydro-powered Norwegian site running Nvidia Vera Rubin systems, operated with Bitdeer.

The deal

Anthropic has committed roughly $10 billion over six years to Volta Infra Holdings, a cloud provider that did not exist at the start of 2026. The capacity comes from a 133-megawatt, hydroelectrically powered data center at Bitdeer Technologies' Tydal campus in Norway — Bitdeer is a bitcoin miner repurposing its power and siting expertise for AI — and is equipped with Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems; reporting on the contract puts the Vera Rubin capacity Anthropic has actually contracted at around 121 megawatts of that site. Handover happens in two even phases, the first targeted for the end of 2026 and the second for March 2027.

Volta was founded in January by former Brookfield asset managers and has raised about $300 million across its seed and Series A rounds, co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Altimeter Capital, with Nvidia, Michael Dell's family office, Azora and Matter Venture Partners also participating, at a $2.4 billion post-money valuation. It sits in Nvidia's Cloud Partner program, has set up a separate $5 billion financing programme with Azora — an asset manager with more than $20 billion under management — to fund its next generation of sites with non-dilutive infrastructure capital and give customers credit-supported access to GPU capacity, and says it has secured roughly a gigawatt of near-term power, with expansion targeted at Texas and Wyoming.

Anthropic's spread

The contract extends a deliberate diversification. Anthropic already has capacity arrangements spanning Google and Broadcom, Amazon, Microsoft Azure, AMD and SpaceX, and is now willing to underwrite a startup's first major buildout to add more. For Volta, a single anchor tenant of this size effectively converts a six-month-old company into a financeable infrastructure business — which is the model: the offtake contract is the collateral.

Why it matters

This is what the compute crunch looks like in practice. Frontier labs are no longer choosing among established hyperscalers; they are seeding new ones, and doing it where power is available rather than where the customers are — Norwegian hydro, at a moment when Texas has just frozen new grid connections. It also deepens the circularity that has drawn scrutiny all year: Nvidia is simultaneously Volta's investor and its chip supplier, while Anthropic's commitment is what makes Volta's Nvidia purchases bankable. That structure works while AI demand grows and amplifies losses through several balance sheets at once if it does not. A $10 billion obligation from a company whose revenue is measured in billions, not tens of billions, is a bet on demand years out.
