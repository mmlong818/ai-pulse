Google brings autonomous business calls into Gemini Tasks

Google says Duplex now powers Gemini Tasks, letting the assistant call businesses and handle real-world requests through a unified Gemini stack.

What happened

Google Research detailed how Duplex evolved from a narrow system for scripted business calls into a broader voice-agent layer inside Gemini Tasks. The company says the feature can call businesses on a user’s behalf to handle practical requests such as checking availability, confirming prices or arranging appointments.

The system must manage fast, irregular conversations in which people interrupt, revise answers and use tone to signal turn-taking. Google describes the current architecture as a unified stack built with Gemini foundation models, rather than the earlier collection of closed-domain models used for specific call types.

Duplex has also been used behind Google Search and Maps. Google says business information gathered through its calls has appeared in Search and Maps more than one trillion times, a scale that illustrates why automated calling is not only a consumer convenience but also a data-collection channel. Earlier Duplex capabilities also contributed to Pixel features such as Call Screen and Hold for Me.

From demo to infrastructure

The announcement is less about a single new model than about productizing a difficult interface: an AI system that must negotiate with people who did not initiate a conversation with a machine. That requires low-latency speech recognition, contextual reasoning, natural synthesis and safeguards around disclosure and consent.

Google says foundation models have enabled Duplex to expand from tightly bounded reservations into more varied tasks across Search, Maps and Gemini. The practical question is how reliably the system handles exceptions, business-specific policies and requests that need confirmation before an action is taken.

Why it matters

Voice agents become consequential when they can complete errands rather than merely answer questions. By moving Duplex into Gemini Tasks, Google is turning a decade of calling research into a general-purpose action layer tied to its maps and merchant data. That could give Gemini a useful advantage in real-world coordination, but it also increases the importance of accurate disclosure, call records and clear boundaries around decisions made for users.
