Anthropic Reports $17 Billion Revenue Ahead of IPO

Anthropic’s amended filing shows explosive revenue growth alongside enormous compute commitments, sharpening questions about frontier AI economics.

Explosive growth, extraordinary commitments

Anthropic reported $17.0 billion in revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, according to an amended filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. Revenue was up from $1.4 billion in the same quarter a year earlier, while the company’s operating loss narrowed to $567 million.

The headline net loss was far larger at $23.3 billion, but the filing makes clear that much of the gap came from non-cash changes in the value of convertible notes and related financial instruments. Anthropic also reported $2.5 billion in adjusted operating income, though its adjusted operating margin declined to 15% as short-term compute became more expensive.

The company’s infrastructure commitments are the more consequential disclosure. Anthropic says it has committed approximately $895.4 billion in purchases and leases, including about $447.5 billion expected before the end of 2030. Nearly $70 billion is due within the following 12 months. Compute and infrastructure expenses reached $18.8 billion during the first nine months of 2026, up 329% year over year.

Anthropic is increasingly pursuing a hybrid model: cloud contracts, directly leased chips, and dedicated facilities. The strategy is designed to secure capacity before demand peaks, but it also ties the company to a long-duration capital plan whose returns depend on continued growth in Claude usage.

Why it matters

The filing turns Anthropic’s IPO into a test of whether frontier-model revenue can justify infrastructure commitments approaching a trillion dollars. Strong demand is evident, but the scale of future obligations means investors will be underwriting both a software business and a massive compute procurement machine.

Uncle Cat take

Anthropic’s $895 billion commitment is the serious story; the $23.3 billion loss is mostly accounting noise, but the capacity bet is not.
