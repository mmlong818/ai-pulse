Google Expands Lyria 3.5 Across Gemini and API

Google has moved its latest music model into global consumer and developer channels, widening access beyond its earlier Flow Music launch.

From a specialist tool to Google’s broader ecosystem

Google has expanded Lyria 3.5 to the Gemini app worldwide and made the music-generation model available through Google AI Studio and the Gemini API. It is also offered in Google Vids and Flow Music, giving consumers, developers and workplace users access through separate creation surfaces.

The underlying model was first introduced in Flow Music in July, so the September event is an access expansion rather than a new model debut. What changed inside the edition window is distribution: Gemini users on the web and mobile can now describe or select a genre, choose vocal or instrumental output, start from templates and request short or longer tracks. API access lets developers embed the same model into their own applications and automated creative workflows.

Google describes Lyria 3.5 as producing richer arrangements, more expressive vocals and higher-fidelity output than its previous music models. It also emphasizes improved pronunciation, lyric structure and control over tempo and duration. Those capabilities address recurring weaknesses in generated music, where a convincing short excerpt may fail to preserve structure or vocal clarity across a complete track. Google’s announcement did not provide a detailed new independent evaluation or disclose training-data composition, leaving comparative quality and rights-management questions open.

Why it matters

Global Gemini and API availability gives Lyria something specialist music generators cannot easily reproduce: placement inside an existing consumer assistant, developer platform and workplace-video product at the same time. That could normalize generated music as a routine component for videos, advertising and personalized media rather than a destination product. The strategic question is whether Google can turn that reach into sustained creator use while giving musicians and commercial buyers sufficient clarity about provenance, permitted uses and generated vocals.
