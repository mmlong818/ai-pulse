OpenAI pushes AI agents deeper into physical engineering

OpenAI says GPT-6 Astra and Codex now support CAD, simulation and manufacturing workflows, while human engineers still validate every design.

From prompts to production

OpenAI says its hardware and infrastructure teams are using ChatGPT and Codex across mechanical design, electrical engineering, simulation and manufacturing preparation. The company also described a test in which GPT-6 Astra helped two employees design a small device for speaking with Codex. Astra produced the enclosure, PCB layout, software integrations and interface; a contract manufacturer built prototypes in less than a month.

The result was not autonomous manufacturing. OpenAI says the design required several rounds of human review because Astra did not consistently account for production tolerances, assembly constraints or the limits of the manufacturer. That qualification matters: the system generated a workable starting point, but did not remove engineering responsibility.

OpenAI is also working with Chip Ganassi Racing to turn 3D scans into editable CAD models and manufacturing drawings. With Mazda, it is testing aerodynamic and crash-safety workflows in which models propose vehicle shapes, simulate airflow and help engineers compare alternatives. OpenAI says a newer internal model scored 0.875 on its BenchCAD 2.0 evaluation, compared with 0.594 for GPT-6 Astra at its highest reasoning setting.

Why it matters

The announcement expands the competitive frontier beyond software agents. If models can reliably coordinate CAD, simulation and manufacturing data, they could reduce the cost of exploring physical designs in automotive, robotics and industrial equipment. But the evidence remains an internal demonstration and partner pilot, not proof of deployment-ready engineering autonomy. The immediate strategic value is likely workflow compression: more candidate designs can be evaluated before expensive physical testing begins.

Uncle Cat take

Astra’s manufacturing-tolerance failures are the key fact: OpenAI has shortened design iteration, but the factory floor still decides whether the model’s work is real.
