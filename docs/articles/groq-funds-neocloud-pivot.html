Groq Raises $650M to Expand Its AI Inference Cloud

Groq’s latest official financing was a $650 million round co-led by Disruptive and Infinitum, supporting expansion of its global inference cloud.

New capital for the inference cloud

Groq announced $650 million in new growth capital on June 22, 2026, co-led by Disruptive and Infinitum. The company said the financing would accelerate expansion of its global AI inference cloud and help it scale toward 200 megawatts of capacity by the end of 2027.

Groq reported that it was operating 13 data centers across North America, Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific, serving more than five million developers and processing trillions of tokens each week. The financing supports the expansion of that cloud footprint rather than a newly announced $350 million round.

Nvidia was not named as a participant in the $650 million financing. Its relationship with Groq comes from a separate transaction announced in December 2025: a non-exclusive agreement licensing Groq’s inference technology to Nvidia. Under that agreement, Groq founder Jonathan Ross, president Sunny Madra and other team members joined Nvidia, while Groq continued operating as an independent company and GroqCloud remained available.

Nvidia subsequently incorporated Groq technology into its LPX inference platform. Groq said its new capital would help fit out its existing infrastructure with its latest inference technology, including the Nvidia LPX system.

Why it matters

The financing reinforces Groq’s focus on selling managed inference capacity rather than relying only on standalone accelerator sales. A cloud service gives developers direct access to Groq-derived technology while placing responsibility for infrastructure, scheduling and utilization on the operator.

That approach can make specialized inference hardware easier to adopt, but it is capital intensive. Expanding capacity requires data-center space, power, networking and equipment before demand is fully realized.

The separate financing and Nvidia licensing arrangements also illustrate a broader consolidation of the AI infrastructure stack. Groq remains an independent inference-cloud operator, while technology it developed is also being incorporated into Nvidia systems. Its next challenge is to translate the additional capital and broader distribution of its technology into reliable capacity and durable customer demand.
