AMD Agrees to Buy World Labs for $8.2 Billion

AMD will acquire Fei-Fei Li’s spatial-intelligence startup in an all-stock deal, bringing world-model research directly into its hardware strategy.

The deal

AMD has agreed to acquire World Labs, the spatial-intelligence company founded by Stanford professor Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $8.2 billion. The deal is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approval.

World Labs will join AMD with its research and engineering team. Li will become AMD’s executive vice president and chief scientist, while World Labs executives Justin Johnson and Ben Mildenhall are also expected to take senior roles.

Why World Labs matters

World Labs has focused on models that understand and generate persistent three-dimensional environments rather than treating images as isolated pixels. Its technology is aimed at applications including robotics, simulation, spatial computing and interactive content.

For AMD, the acquisition is a significant move beyond selling accelerators. The company is trying to build a deeper relationship between future AI models and the chips, memory systems and software used to run them. World-model workloads could create new requirements for graphics, simulation and inference hardware, particularly in robotics and physical AI.

The transaction is also notable because AMD is paying a very large price for a relatively young research company. The value reflects the scarcity of teams working on spatial intelligence, but it also makes execution and product integration important questions.

Why it matters

The deal signals that competition in AI infrastructure is expanding from language models toward systems that model physical space and the real world. If AMD can turn World Labs’ research into deployable workloads, it could improve its position against Nvidia in the next generation of physical AI. If the research remains difficult to commercialize, the purchase will look more like a high-cost bet on an uncertain model category.

Uncle Cat take: AMD is paying $8.2 billion for a bridge between world models and hardware; the price assumes spatial AI becomes infrastructure, not a research niche.
