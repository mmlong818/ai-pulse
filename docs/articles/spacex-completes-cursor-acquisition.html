SpaceX Completes $60 Billion Acquisition of AI Coding Firm Cursor

Cursor has formally joined SpaceXAI in an all-stock acquisition valuing the coding-agent company at approximately $60 billion.

The deal closes

Cursor said it has formally become part of SpaceX after the companies completed their acquisition. Although Cursor’s closing announcement did not restate the financial terms, SpaceX’s earlier regulatory disclosure described an all-stock transaction with an implied equity value of approximately $60 billion for Cursor. Cursor’s team will join SpaceXAI and work on Grok, Grok Build, Grok Bot, the Grok API and Cursor, turning an earlier technical partnership into full organizational integration.

The transaction follows an April compute and option agreement that gave SpaceX the right, but not the obligation, to acquire Cursor at a predetermined valuation. SpaceX exercised that option in June, and the merger subsequently closed in August. Cursor had already begun using SpaceXAI’s Colossus infrastructure to expand training for its Composer coding models, saying that access to computing capacity had become a constraint on further scaling. Closing the acquisition removes the corporate boundary between Cursor’s coding products, its model-development operation and SpaceXAI’s infrastructure.

Cursor has evolved from an AI-assisted code editor into a broader agent platform. Its products increasingly delegate multi-step software work to cloud agents, while its internal research has expanded beyond code completion into reinforcement learning, agent coordination and dedicated foundation models. The acquisition therefore gives SpaceXAI more than a popular developer interface: it gains an established distribution channel among programmers, an experienced agent engineering group and a high-value environment in which new Grok models can be trained and evaluated.

Strategic consequences

The closing announcement does not fully explain whether Cursor will retain independent branding over the long term, how customer data will be separated, or whether users will continue to receive models from competing providers. Those questions are commercially important because Cursor’s appeal has partly rested on giving developers access to multiple model families rather than binding them to one laboratory.

The deal matters because it vertically integrates three scarce AI assets: frontier-scale compute, model development and a widely used professional application. Coding agents generate unusually rich feedback about whether models can plan, operate tools and complete verifiable work. Bringing that feedback loop inside SpaceXAI could accelerate Grok’s development while placing one of the most influential AI-development interfaces under the control of a frontier-model owner.
