Google Opens SynthID Detector for Public AI Content Checks

Google is opening its SynthID verification portal globally, allowing anyone to check selected image, video and audio files for watermarks.

What happened

Google DeepMind said SynthID Detector is now available to everyone through a public verification portal. The service checks whether images, video or audio contain an invisible SynthID watermark associated with content generated or altered by Google AI and participating partners.

Google says SynthID watermarks are designed to survive common edits such as filters and other transformations. The detector does not identify every piece of synthetic media; it can only confirm the presence of a compatible watermark. Google said the partner set includes OpenAI, NVIDIA and Kakao, with Apple expected to join later.

The company said more than 180 billion images and videos now carry SynthID watermarks. The public launch follows an earlier period of restricted testing with journalists and media professionals. Users can upload content to the portal, while related detection is also available through Gemini, Search and Chrome in supported situations.

Why it matters

Opening the detector turns provenance from a lab feature into a public utility, but it does not solve the broader identification problem. Watermark coverage depends on whether a generator embeds SynthID, and a positive result says more about participating production pipelines than about the entire history of a file. The move nevertheless establishes a practical infrastructure layer for newsrooms, platforms and investigators. Its influence will depend on whether major image, video and audio providers adopt compatible marks and whether verification results can be incorporated into publishing workflows without being treated as absolute proof.
