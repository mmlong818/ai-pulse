Replit CEO Pitches the Rise of Self-Driving Companies

Replit’s CEO argues that AI agents will let companies operate through increasingly autonomous software teams, shifting the debate from coding tools to work design.

The argument

Replit CEO Amjad Masad has described the “self-driving company” as a future in which software agents handle much more of the routine work currently divided among employees, tools, and internal processes. The idea extends beyond AI-assisted coding: agents would create applications, maintain workflows, respond to operational requests, and coordinate with one another across a company’s systems.

Replit is a useful vantage point for this argument because its product already sits between natural-language software creation and deployment. The company’s broader strategy has been to make building and operating small applications accessible to people who are not traditional programmers. Masad’s thesis pushes that direction further, treating the company itself as an executable system whose processes can increasingly be delegated to agents.

What changes

If the model works, the unit of automation moves from an individual task to a business process. A support request might trigger an agent to inspect records, write a small tool, ask for approval, and update the customer system. A product idea could move from description to prototype, test, and deployment with fewer handoffs. That could matter especially for small companies that lack dedicated engineering and operations teams.

The claim remains much larger than the evidence available today. Agents still struggle with ambiguous goals, hidden dependencies, permissions, and responsibility when an action has consequences. “Autonomous” workflows also require monitoring, rollback, identity controls, and reliable records of why a system acted. Those costs do not disappear because the interface is conversational.

The significance of Masad’s framing is strategic rather than predictive. It captures where AI software competition is moving: from making one worker faster toward redesigning how work is organised. The winners will be determined by whether agents can operate safely inside real company constraints, not by how persuasive the self-driving metaphor sounds on stage.
