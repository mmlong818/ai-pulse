Anthropic begins Nasdaq trading in landmark AI IPO

Anthropic’s public debut turns frontier-model development into a public-market test of safety promises, spending needs, and investor appetite. The offering priced at $83 a share and, after the fully exercised over-allotment option, comprised 1.127 billion Class A shares, including 1,103,351,354 sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 sold by existing stockholders. Shares closed their first trading day at $97.17, roughly 17% above the IPO price.

Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq on Nov. 19 under the symbol ANT, completing one of the most consequential public-market events in the AI industry. The company priced its initial offering at $83 a share. After the underwriters fully exercised their option to purchase an additional 147 million shares from Anthropic, the offering comprised 1.127 billion Class A shares: Anthropic sold 1,103,351,354 shares and certain existing holders sold another 23,648,646. At the IPO price, the total public offering represented about $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, of which about $91.6 billion went to Anthropic before underwriting discounts and expenses. Anthropic also closed a concurrent private placement of about $26 billion in Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners. Shares opened at $111.50 and closed the first trading session at $97.17, about 17% above the IPO price.

A new financing regime for frontier labs

The IPO gives Anthropic access to a much broader pool of capital as it competes to train and serve increasingly expensive models. Its public-benefit-corporation structure and stated safety commitments now sit alongside quarterly disclosure requirements, shareholder scrutiny, and pressure to show that enormous compute bills can become durable revenue.

The structure also makes the company’s capital needs easier to observe. Anthropic’s founders reportedly sold nearly $2 billion worth of shares, while the company itself receives the proceeds from the primary portion of the offering. That distinction matters: a public listing can create liquidity for early holders without automatically adding the same amount of cash to model development.

Why it matters

Anthropic is the first major frontier-model lab of this wave to face the public market at scale. Its performance will influence how investors value other AI labs, how much autonomy safety-focused companies retain, and whether the sector’s spending trajectory looks like a temporary race or a sustainable technology business. The unresolved question is not whether Anthropic can raise money, but whether public ownership will reward long-horizon safety investment when model releases, infrastructure costs, and commercial returns move on different clocks.

Uncle Cat take

Anthropic’s first-day close below its opening price but well above the $83 IPO price makes its governance model measurable: safety language now has to survive quarterly capital-market pressure.
