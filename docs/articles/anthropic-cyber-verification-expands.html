Anthropic Expands Verified Access to Cyber Models

Anthropic is widening its Cyber Verification Program, giving more vetted security teams access to capable models under reduced defensive safeguards.

Anthropic is expanding its Cyber Verification Program to give more security professionals access to its most capable models for defensive work. The company says verified participants will receive broader access under reduced cyber safeguards, with eligibility tied to security verification rather than general public availability.

The program is aimed at teams conducting vulnerability research, incident response and other work where ordinary model refusals can interfere with legitimate investigation. Anthropic’s announcement follows a broader shift among frontier labs toward differentiated access: the same models may be constrained for ordinary users while vetted organizations receive additional capability for high-stakes defensive tasks.

Anthropic has not described this as an unrestricted cyber release. The program is gated, and the company says access is intended for verified security professionals. That leaves important operational questions open, including how applicants are checked, how usage is monitored, how incidents are handled and whether the additional capability can remain restricted once outputs move beyond the original organization.

The timing is significant because frontier models are increasingly useful for both vulnerability discovery and remediation. Expanding access could improve defenders’ ability to reproduce flaws, analyze malware and write detection logic, but it also increases the consequences of account compromise, insider misuse or poorly bounded automation.

Why it matters: Anthropic is treating cyber capability as a controlled professional service rather than a binary allow-or-refuse feature. The program’s credibility will depend less on the announcement than on whether verification and monitoring can scale without turning defensive access into a new route for offensive misuse.
