Trump orders US agencies to call AI superintelligence

The White House directed US officials to replace artificial intelligence with superintelligence in government language, turning terminology into policy messaging.

What happened

President Donald Trump ordered the US government to refer to artificial intelligence as “super intelligence,” according to a directive reported on September 29. The instruction applies to government communications and reflects the administration’s effort to frame AI as a strategic capability rather than a conventional software category.

The wording change arrives as Washington is expanding its involvement in frontier AI. The administration is courting major technology companies, discussing voluntary oversight arrangements and positioning advanced models as part of national competitiveness and security policy. The terminology does not itself create a new model rule, funding program or safety standard, but it gives agencies a common political vocabulary for describing the technology.

Why it matters

Government language can influence how agencies classify priorities, write procurement documents and communicate risk. “Artificial intelligence” is a broad technical term that covers everything from recommendation systems to frontier models. “Super intelligence” implies a narrower and more dramatic category, one associated with systems that could exceed human performance across many domains.

That framing may help the administration justify faster investment and stronger coordination around advanced systems. It may also blur important distinctions between current capabilities and hypothetical future systems. Treating all AI as superintelligence risks making policy less precise, especially when regulators need to distinguish model training, deployment, automated decision-making and ordinary software.

The immediate effect is therefore rhetorical, not regulatory. Its significance lies in the direction it signals: the US government is increasingly presenting frontier AI as a national strategic asset whose development deserves exceptional attention. Whether that produces coherent policy will depend on whether the language is followed by measurable standards, budgets and agency responsibilities.

Uncle Cat take

Replacing a technical category with “super intelligence” may strengthen Washington’s strategic message, but it gives agencies no operational test for deciding which systems qualify.
