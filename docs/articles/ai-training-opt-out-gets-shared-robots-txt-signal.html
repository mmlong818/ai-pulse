Google backs a shared robots.txt training opt-out

Google宣布计划支持一种基于IETF AIPREF工作的通用robots.txt信号，帮助网站向多个参与的AI服务商表达训练偏好。

What happened

Google announced plans to support a shared policy token for robots.txt that would let websites express broad AI-training preferences across participating providers instead of maintaining separate instructions for every crawler. Google said that when a site uses the shared token to opt out of training and does not explicitly specify Google-Extended, it plans to interpret the signal as a disallow for Google-Extended.

The proposal comes from work in the IETF’s AIPREF process. Google’s announcement describes the mechanism as an industry signal intended for adoption by multiple AI providers, but it does not itself establish that Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic have all formally adopted the same control. Google says it aims to support the mechanism alongside other AI providers in 2027.

Why it matters

The move addresses a practical problem for publishers: today’s opt-out controls differ by provider and may distinguish training, search grounding, and product access. A shared token could lower the technical burden for smaller websites and make publisher preferences easier for participating crawlers to interpret consistently.

The announcement is not a licensing settlement and does not force every AI company or crawler to comply. It also leaves important questions open, including which providers will implement the signal, how enforcement will be documented, how conflicts between broad and provider-specific rules will be resolved, and whether opting out of training will affect search or answer-generation products separately.

For the AI industry, the significance is institutional rather than immediate. If the signal becomes widely implemented, it could become basic web infrastructure for negotiating access to public content. If adoption remains voluntary and uneven, publishers may still need a patchwork of vendor-specific controls.

Uncle Cat take

The useful deliverable is the proposed shared token, but its value depends on adoption and crawler compliance records that have not yet been promised.
