Anthropic expands Claude into collaborative office work

Claude gains Whiteboards, Sheets and Data tools as Anthropic pushes beyond coding into shared planning, analysis and workplace execution.

Anthropic has expanded Claude with a set of workplace features that turn the assistant into a more persistent collaborative workspace. The company launched Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets in beta, made Claude Data generally available, and announced Claude Meetings as a forthcoming feature designed to participate in calls and convert discussions into follow-up work.

A broader working surface

Whiteboards lets users talk through an unfinished idea and have Claude organize it into a visual plan with timelines, owners and deadlines. Sheets allows the assistant to create and edit trackers, budgets and other structured workbooks. Claude Data, formerly called Dashboards, adds certified queries, data stories and narrated presentations built from a team’s metrics.

Anthropic is also expanding interactive connectors. Users can open fuller versions of connected tools from inside a Claude conversation, including integrations involving Adobe, Atlassian and Box. The aim is to keep context from being scattered across email, documents, spreadsheets and specialist applications.

Claude Meetings is described by Anthropic as coming soon. The company says it will let users add Claude to a call to take notes and update documents during the conversation. It should therefore be treated as an announced feature rather than a generally available meeting participant. Questions around consent, recording, access permissions and automatically generated tasks will matter if and when it launches.

The rollout of Whiteboards, Sheets and Data is available across paid plans, although enterprise administrators can control some features. Whiteboards and Sheets are off by default for Enterprise, while Claude Data is scheduled to turn on by default for those customers on November 19.

Why it matters

Anthropic is competing for the layer where work is organized, not only the layer where text is generated. The company’s advantage may come from adapting Claude Code’s context-and-artifact workflow to nontechnical teams. Its unresolved challenge is governance: a board, spreadsheet or meeting transcript can become an action system, and mistakes in ownership, permissions or data interpretation may travel directly into business operations.
