Meta adds customizable Spaces to its Muse AI agent

Meta’s new Muse Spaces let users turn prompts into task-specific environments, pushing personal AI agents toward persistent, reusable workflows.

What happened

Meta introduced Spaces inside the Muse app on November 6. Users can describe an idea—such as a gift tracker, daily word game or project-planning board—and Muse creates a customized interactive environment around it.

The feature moves Muse beyond a conventional chat interface. Instead of asking an assistant to answer one request at a time, users can create a reusable workspace with its own purpose, tools and interaction pattern. Meta describes the experience as something people design for the moment they need it.

Why it matters

Spaces is an important product direction because it treats an AI agent as an adaptable interface rather than a single general-purpose assistant. That approach could help Meta compete for recurring user behavior: a successful space may become a destination that people revisit, modify and share instead of a conversation that disappears into a chat history.

The launch also extends Meta’s broader Muse strategy, which emphasizes proactive assistance and personalized experiences. However, the announcement does not yet establish how far Spaces can go in accessing external services, retaining state, collaborating with other users or executing consequential actions. Those details will determine whether the feature becomes a genuine agent platform or remains a collection of prompt-generated mini-apps.

Uncle Cat take

Meta’s strongest move here is the product container, not the generated novelty: if Spaces retain useful state and become shareable, Muse gains a path from chatbot to user-owned workflow.
