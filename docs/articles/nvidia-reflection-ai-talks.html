Nvidia Weighs Deeper Tie-Up With Reflection AI

Nvidia is reportedly considering an acquisition, acqui-hire or larger investment in Reflection AI, tightening its control over open-model talent.

Nvidia is in early discussions about acquiring Reflection AI, hiring much of its team or increasing its investment in the open-weight model developer, according to reporting cited by the Financial Times. The talks could still fail, and neither company has publicly confirmed them.

Several possible deal structures

The reported options range from a conventional acquisition to an acqui-hire that would bring Reflection’s employees and technology into Nvidia while allowing parts of the startup to continue operating. A larger financing round, potentially paired with expanded access to Nvidia computing capacity, is another possibility.

Nvidia already led Reflection’s $2 billion financing round in 2025 and has reportedly invested about $800 million in total. Reflection recently disclosed Beam, a 501-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model aimed at coding agents and other tool-using workloads. Its training depends heavily on Nvidia hardware, including GB300 systems supplied through a large capacity agreement involving SpaceX’s data-center business.

Why the talks matter

The potential transaction would extend Nvidia’s strategy beyond selling accelerators. By taking a deeper position in a company building open-weight models and coding agents, Nvidia could gain influence over both the hardware demand curve and the software systems that determine how that hardware is used.

It would also test how much independence remains in the open-model ecosystem when leading developers depend on a small number of infrastructure suppliers. Reflection’s reported $25 billion pre-money valuation and its recent Beam launch make the situation more than a routine strategic investment. At the same time, the uncertain status of the talks means readers should treat any claim of a completed acquisition as premature.
