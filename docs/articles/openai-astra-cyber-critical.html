OpenAI Launches Cyber-Critical GPT-6 Astra

OpenAI has broadly released GPT-6 Astra with tighter safeguards and restricted access to its most advanced cybersecurity capabilities.

A critical-capability model reaches users

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, two days after announcing that it had reached the “Critical” cybersecurity threshold under the company’s Preparedness Framework. It is the first OpenAI model to receive that classification. According to OpenAI, Astra can, with appropriate tools and access, discover previously unknown vulnerabilities and develop exploits across many well-protected systems without a person guiding every step.

The release supersedes OpenAI’s earlier description of Astra as forthcoming. The company now calls it its most capable broadly deployed model and has published a safety overview and full system card. Access to Astra’s most advanced cybersecurity capabilities remains more limited than ordinary use, with less restrictive defensive access expected to expand through OpenAI’s Daybreak program.

OpenAI says it delayed parts of Astra’s development and release while strengthening safeguards. Measures include stricter isolation, checkpoint encryption, monitoring of complete action trajectories, alignment checks before internal use, stronger refusals of harmful cyber requests and systems capable of stopping potentially unauthorized activity. The company also incorporated lessons from the OpenAI–Hugging Face infrastructure incident, although Astra itself was not used in that episode.

The newly published material adds evidence absent from the initial announcement. OpenAI reports that Astra scored 100% on ExploitBench and discovered two previously unknown V8 vulnerabilities while working through an internal benchmark. Those results remain developer-reported, and sensitive details are necessarily withheld while disclosure to maintainers proceeds. OpenAI also warns that added safeguards may slow, pause or stop legitimate work when activity is flagged as possible misuse or unauthorized behavior.

Why it matters

A frontier developer has now broadly deployed a model it says can autonomously find and operationalize unknown weaknesses, while segmenting the most sensitive capabilities behind additional controls. This shifts Astra from a prospective safety case to a live test of whether cyber-critical capability can be distributed without becoming a broadly reusable offensive service. Independent scrutiny will now focus on both real-world capability and the rate at which safeguards interrupt legitimate defensive work.
